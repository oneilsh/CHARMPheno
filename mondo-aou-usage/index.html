Counting Diagnoses: AllOfUs on Mondo

Diagnosis counts from the AllOfUs EHR database (v8) mapped to the Mondo disease and HPO phenotype ontologies. Counts of 20 or fewer have been masked; similar counts may be found at the AllOfUs Public Data Browser. Methods detailsEach participant's coded conditions in the AllOfUs condition_occurrence table are routed to a single best-home term by a ladder, taking the most specific reachable: (1) the source code (e.g. ICD) is one of a Mondo term's same as cross-references; else (2) its standardized SNOMED concept maps to a Mondo term via OMOP Maps to; else (3) the concept is an exact HPO phenotype term (used when Mondo has no specific term); else (4) it is rolled up the SNOMED hierarchy to the nearest Mondo ancestor. Mondo (diseases) is preferred, so HPO (phenotypes) claims only what Mondo would otherwise over-generalize — headache, nausea, lab abnormalities, and the like. Each participant is credited once per term; counts of 1–20 are masked and shown as ≤20.The two ontologies are drawn together but unlinked: Mondo diseases grow rightward from the center, HPO phenotypes leftward, their roots meeting in the middle. Both keep their real branch structure — a grouping term with no code of its own is retained when it is a genuine branch point (two or more children lead to used terms); purely linear pass-through levels are collapsed.A single coded condition can legitimately reach several terms in one ontology at once — its SNOMED concept is shared by several terms (OMOP's ICD→SNOMED Maps to is one-to-many), or it rolled up to several equally-near ancestors in Mondo's poly-hierarchy. So a term's own count answers "how many participants touch this term," and these per-term counts are not meant to be added together. When terms are combined in a subtree roll-up, each shared diagnosis is instead split 1/m across the m terms it reaches (fractional attribution), so the rolled-up total is de-double-counted and honestly additive.Two node-size views are available. Per-term sizes each node by its own count, so a broad classification can look small while its specific children are large. Rolled up sizes each node by its de-double-counted subtree total — a diagnosis count (a participant with several diagnoses under one branch counts once per diagnosis), reported as a central estimate ± small-cell suppression.Note that many Mondo disease entries do not have corresponding codes that would be found in an EHR; others do, but there is no direct mapping between them. Small counts or counts of 0 should thus be interpreted as absence of evidence, rather than evidence of absence.
