# Proposal — a threshold-free, uncertainty-aware "presence" quantity from the model itself

> **SUPERSEDED (2026-07-06) by
> `2026-07-06-predictive-gain-presence-depth-prominence-design.md`.** Fable's second review pivoted
> the metric from a functional of the (biased) point posterior to a predictive CONTRAST
> (leave-one-topic-out held-out predictive gain), which dissolves the floor / uncertainty / saturation
> patches instead of adding to them. This doc is kept as the record of how we got there (and carries
> Fable's first review); the predictive-gain doc is the design we build.

Companion to update 4. Data shape as before: documents are user-day bag-of-words aggregates; a
gated logistic-normal topic model (background block ∪ per-group foreground blocks, hard-masked);
per-document θ = softmax(η), η ~ Normal(Γᵀx, Σ). We want to report, per topic k, "what fraction of
documents express topic k" — and we want the model to supply the definition instead of a hand-set
threshold.

## 1. The problem with a threshold on θ̂

The obvious readout is "fraction of documents with θ̂_k > τ." Two failures:

- **τ is arbitrary.** With K topics the uniform value is 1/K, and a τ just above it (our default sat
  barely above uniform) sweeps up the entire shoulder of the distribution; a τ further out is
  equally unmotivated. Every choice is a knob to defend.
- **θ̂_k is blind to content.** A document can carry an elevated θ̂_k purely from the prior mean
  (Γᵀx) while containing NONE of topic k's characteristic tokens. Thresholding θ̂ counts it anyway.
  This is what makes common topics all pile up at a similar, uninformative rate: the metric measures
  an abstract mixing weight, not whether the document actually looks like topic k.

## 2. The quantity the model already implies: per-token responsibilities

The generative model attributes each token, not just each document. For a document with token w and
weights θ, the posterior that THIS token was generated by topic k is the responsibility

    resp_k(w) = θ_k · β_k,w / Σ_j θ_j · β_j,w

a genuine probability, and — crucially — it is large only when β_k,w is large, i.e. only when the
token is characteristic of topic k. A document with a prior-inflated θ_k but none of k's words gets
resp_k ≈ 0 on all its tokens. So responsibilities restore the content-grounding that thresholding θ̂
threw away.

## 3. Threshold-free presence: the probability of ≥1 attributable token

Define "document d expresses topic k" as the event that at least one of its tokens was generated by
k. Under the model (given θ), tokens are attributed independently, so

    P(d expresses k | θ) = 1 − Π_w (1 − resp_k(w))^{n_w}

and the corpus **presence rate** of topic k is the mean of this over documents. It is threshold-free
(the "≥1 token" event is intrinsic to the generative process, not a tuned cutoff), it is grounded in
tokens the document actually contains, and it differentiates topics naturally: a narrow topic whose
β concentrates on a few tokens is "present" only in documents that carry those tokens, so its rate
is low and MEANS something; a broad topic is present more widely, honestly. (A close variant reports
the expected NUMBER of attributable tokens, E[count_k] = Σ_w n_w·resp_k(w), and thresholds it at 1
"at least one token"; the probability form above is the cleaner statement and the two nearly agree
when responsibilities are small.)

## 4. The uncertainty enhancement (the part worth doing properly)

Everything above plugs in θ̂ — a point (the MAP). But a 3-token document and a 300-token document
should not speak with the same confidence, and the model already quantifies this: the logistic-
normal E-step returns a Laplace posterior over η (a Gaussian, mean η̂, covariance the inverse
Hessian at the mode), hence a posterior over θ. Integrate the presence probability over it:

    P(d expresses k) = E_{θ ~ posterior(d)} [ 1 − Π_w (1 − resp_k(w; θ))^{n_w} ]

estimated by drawing S samples of η from the Laplace Gaussian, mapping to θ, and averaging. The
effect is exactly the right one: a sparse document has a WIDE θ posterior, so its presence
probability is pulled toward the prior expectation (it is not counted as confidently); a rich
document has a TIGHT posterior and speaks with near-certainty. The corpus presence rate becomes an
uncertainty-weighted quantity — no document's thin evidence is inflated into a hard "has it."

This is the posterior-predictive presence rate: no free threshold, the model's own attribution, the
model's own uncertainty. It is the same "let the calibrated posterior, not a hand-set constant,
supply the number" move as the scale calibration — applied one level down, to membership.

## 5. What it surfaces

- **A real, differentiated rate per topic**, on the same footing as the corpus's own generative
  process, replacing the last hand-tuned knob in the readout.
- **Automatic honesty on thin documents** — the uncertainty integration regresses under-evidenced
  documents toward the prior rather than counting them.
- It does NOT force rates down artificially. A topic whose characteristic tokens are genuinely
  common will have a genuinely high presence rate — but now the number is a statement about content
  and evidence, not about where a slider sat.

## 6. Implementation sketch

Reuses the per-document inference pass we already run (the one that produces per-document θ̂ at the
calibrated scale — see update 4; membership MUST use the calibrated scale, not the fit scale, for
the same compression reason). Per document, per topic in its allowed (gated) set: draw S η-samples
from the Laplace Gaussian, compute resp per token per sample, the ≥1-token probability per sample,
average → the document's posterior presence probability. Accumulate per-topic sums across the corpus
(mapPartitions + treeReduce, the same distributed idiom as the scale sweep). Output a per-topic
presence-rate vector (and optionally a per-topic distribution of per-document probabilities, the way
we already ship a θ̂ distribution). Cost is one extra distributed pass with a small S (the Laplace
draw is a (K−1)-dim Gaussian; S ~ 32–64 is plenty for a mean).

## 7. Open questions we'd value your read on

- **Does the uncertainty integration change the ranking materially, or mostly the tails?** Prediction:
  it mainly demotes topics whose apparent presence rode on thin documents; worth measuring the
  ranking shift vs the plug-in-θ̂ version.
- **≥1-token event vs expected-token-count ≥ 1** — which is the more interpretable headline. The
  probability form is cleaner; the count form is more intuitive to a consumer. Maybe report both.
- **Validation by generate-then-re-infer.** On a synthetic corpus with a PLANTED per-topic membership
  (documents generated with/without topic k), does the recovered presence rate match the planted
  rate across topic breadths and document lengths? This is the same generate-then-re-infer discipline
  that validated the scale calibration.
- **Gated structure.** For a foreground topic, membership is partly structural (only its group's
  documents can express it under the hard mask), so its presence rate is naturally a WITHIN-group
  rate. Is the right headline the within-group rate, the whole-corpus rate, or both? (Background
  topics have no such restriction.)
- **Does this dissolve or merely relocate the "broad topics look common" concern?** If a broad topic
  genuinely appears across most documents, a high rate is correct — but is a rate the right summary
  for a broad topic at all, or should breadth itself be reported alongside it?

## Converged design — Fable's review incorporated (2026-07-06)

Fable's response reframed two things, named a confound the first draft missed, and settled the open
questions. This section is the design we build.

### 1. It is TWO coordinates, not one number: PRESENCE × DEPTH

A single rate conflates "how widely" with "how much." Report both, as the two axes they are:

- **Presence** (headline, "how widely") — P(document has ≥1 token attributable to topic k),
  integrated over the θ-posterior (below). Mean over documents = the topic's presence rate.
- **Depth** (companion, "how much") — the attributed SHARE, E[count_k]/n_d = (Σ_w n_w·resp_k(w)) / n_d,
  i.e. the expected fraction of a document's tokens attributed to k, summarized over the documents
  where the topic is PRESENT.

A broad common topic reads high-presence / low-depth; a narrow specific topic reads
low-presence / high-depth. This separates what the θ̂ threshold conflated, and it is the natural
answer to the vis question — topics live in a (presence, depth) plane, not on one axis. It also
dissolves the "≥1-event vs expected-count" open question without a choice: the probability form IS
presence; the normalized count form IS depth. They were never competing definitions of one thing —
they are the two coordinates. Report both.

### 2. The posterior integration is the CORRECTION, not an enhancement

Given θ, token attributions are independent — but θ is NOT given, and marginally the attributions
are correlated through the shared θ uncertainty. For a rare topic the posterior is effectively "either
this document is somewhat about k (θ_k non-trivial, several tokens attribute) or it is not (θ_k ≈ 0,
none do)." The plug-in `1 − Π(1 − resp)` evaluated at a single compromise θ̂ gets exactly these cases
wrong; E_θ[1 − Π(1 − resp(θ))] gets them right, because each posterior sample commits to one branch
of the about-it-or-not split. So the S-sample integration is not a layer on a correct base metric —
it IS the base metric; the plug-in is a biased approximation. It matters MOST for the rare foreground
topics the gated design exists to surface. Consequence for validation: the ranking shift vs plug-in is
not only "thin documents demoted" — rare-topic presence can move in EITHER direction; measure the
per-topic delta (not just rank correlation).

### 3. The named confound: presence saturates with document length

The ≥1-token event is monotone in n and saturates: presence = 1 − (1−r)^n for mean per-token
responsibility r; at n=44, r=0.05 → 0.90, r=0.10 → 0.99. Two consequences: (a) common topics all read
near 1, so presence stops discriminating among them (partly recreating the θ̂-threshold pile-up); (b)
presence covaries with user activity and with any drift in the corpus length distribution — a
length-distribution shift moves presence rates with nothing about the topics changing. The event is
still the right DEFINITION (intrinsic to the generative process, correct semantics for narrow topics);
the fix is the DEPTH companion (§1), which does not saturate, plus reporting length-dependence from
validation (§7) so future presence drift can be attributed.

### 4. Numerics: log-space product + MEASURE the smoothing floor

- **Log space.** Compute log(1 − presence) = Σ_w n_w · log1p(−resp_k(w)); signature tokens push resp
  near 1 and the naive product underflows / loses precision.
- **Smoothing floor.** resp_k(w) is never exactly 0 — β carries Dirichlet-smoothed mass on every
  token, so a per-token floor ε (~1e-3) gives every topic a spurious ~ε·n presence baseline (~4% at
  n=44) uniform across documents containing nothing of the topic. Do NOT assume it negligible: the
  validation (§7) must include topics ABSENT by construction and report the false-presence rate. If
  material, either report it as an explicit floor (consumers read rates relative to it) or compute
  responsibilities from a lightly-truncated β.

### 5. Gated denominator: WITHIN-GROUP is the only foreground headline

whole-corpus rate = group-share × within-group rate, so it confounds topic prevalence with group
size (60% of a group that is 5% of the corpus reads as 3% — a statement about group size, not the
topic). Rules: within-group is the foreground headline; corpus-wide only as an explicitly-labeled
composition view; NEVER rank foreground and background topics in one table on the raw rate (their
denominators differ structurally); a unified view needs the denominator as a COLUMN, not a footnote.
(Background topics have no restriction — their denominator is the whole corpus.)

### 6. The Laplace covariance is honest only at the calibrated scale

The uncertainty integration inherits the scale arc: the Laplace posterior COVARIANCE (not just the
mode) is compressed at the unit fit scale, so it must be computed at the calibrated scale c*. This is
the ADR-0034 addendum applied to the covariance. It silently regresses — pin it in code, not just the
doc.

### 7. Monte-Carlo budget — under the no-per-document-export constraint

Governing constraint: the platform's terms of service prohibit republishing the underlying data or
any individualized (per-document) information — only per-topic AGGREGATES leave, never per-document
quantities. This MOOTS the per-document MC concern (the ~±0.09-at-S=32 per-document error averages
out in the corpus mean). S ≈ 32–64 is therefore plenty for the corpus-level presence/depth means. If
we want to convey the SPREAD of per-document presence, ship an aggregate per-topic presence HISTOGRAM
(a binned summary over documents — the same aggregate treatment the θ̂ histogram already gets), never
the raw per-document values.

### 8. Validation: a FACTORIAL plant

Generate-then-re-infer, with the plant crossed over the axes the confounds identify:
planted-membership × topic-breadth (narrow-signature vs broad-vocabulary β) × document-length
(subsample the real length distribution, INCLUDING the thin tail). Success criteria:
- recovered presence tracks planted presence across ALL cells;
- the false-presence floor (§4) is characterized (topics absent-by-construction);
- the length-dependence of recovered presence at a FIXED planted rate is measured, so future presence
  drift can be attributed to length drift vs topic change.
Add one cell: documents planted with an AMBIGUOUS token (shared between two topics) but NOT the
topic's signature — verifies the θ-weighting in resp behaves as intended (attribution follows document
context), a feature a consumer should be able to see quantified.

### Build order

1. Distributed per-document pass (reuse the calibrated-scale E-step from the θ-histogram work):
   per document, per allowed topic — draw S η-samples from the Laplace Gaussian (at c*), compute resp
   per token (log space), accumulate the per-sample ≥1-token probability AND the per-sample attributed
   share; average → per-document (presence, depth); accumulate per-topic corpus sums (mapPartitions +
   treeReduce). Foreground topics accumulate WITHIN their group.
2. Export: per-topic (presence, depth) AGGREGATES with the correct denominator labeled; optionally an
   aggregate per-topic presence HISTOGRAM (a binned summary over documents — like the θ̂ histogram).
   NO per-document export (the terms of service forbid republishing the data or individualized info)
   — per-topic aggregates only. The three validation diagnostics (§8/real-corpus) are likewise
   computed in-pass and emitted as per-topic aggregates (a floor scalar, a length-correlation scalar,
   a signature-match scalar), never per-document rows.
3. Validation harness: the factorial synthetic plant (§8) with the false-presence-floor readout.
4. Frontend: the (presence, depth) plane as the readout; drop the τ threshold from the headline.
